Model basis: Purchase area is fixed at 150 sq ft regardless of bed count. General Store scales proportionally with the bed multiplier relative to 250 beds, with a hard floor of 100 sq ft (never goes below, even for smaller hospitals). Fluid Store, IP Dispensing, IP Store, and Receiving Area all scale linearly on a sq ft/bed rate derived from the 250-bed base case (Fluid 0.72, Dispensing 0.88, IP Store 2.08, Receiving 1.52 sq ft/bed).

Statutory note: Schedule N of the Drugs & Cosmetics Rules, 1945 separately requires the dispensing area to be at least ~64.6 sq ft for one pharmacist plus ~21.5 sq ft per additional pharmacist — a staffing-based legal floor, independent of this bed-based model. Verify the IP Dispensing figure against actual pharmacist headcount before finalizing.

Compiled from USP <797>/<800> cleanroom rules (international, applies where sterile/hazardous compounding occurs), Schedule N general premises rules (India, applies to all pharmacies), and NABH narcotic-control expectations (India, accreditation-linked)

Cosmetics, visible jewellery (rings, bracelets, watches)Harbour microbes; interfere with glove integrity and aseptic techniqueUSP <800> checklist guidance

Uncalibrated household thermometers/refrigerators for drug or vaccine storageInaccurate readings risk cold-chain failure and product lossCDC vaccine storage toolkit; WHO cold chain guidance

This list is illustrative of the most commonly cited prohibitions and is not exhaustive.
